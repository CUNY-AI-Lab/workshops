## About the CUNY AI Lab

The **CUNY AI Lab** is a faculty and staff-led incubator for experimentation with AI by CUNY researchers across disciplines, strengthening CUNY's position at the leading edge of technological innovation. Located at the CUNY Graduate Center and led by experienced scholars and technologists who have built widely used open publishing platforms, the Lab fosters momentum for AI experimentation while foregrounding ethical practices and values that reinforce CUNY's mission of accessible, equitable, and transformative education.

This presentation documents the Critical AI Literacy Institute, whose T(h)inkering strand is carried out with the CUNY AI Lab.

## The Four Papers

1. **The Critical AI Literacy Institute: Asserting and Preserving Scholarly Agency in the Age of AI** (Luke Waltzer) - The Teaching and Learning Center, CALI's origins and elements, its grounding scholarship, definitions of critical AI literacy (Guest, Suarez, and van Rooij), the CUNY context, goals (reasoned adoption and informed refusal; communities of practice; research, tinker, advocate), funding (a three-year Google.org grant and a second grant through the Empire AI Initiative), and strategy (emphasize agency, identify small teaching moments, ground interventions in critical inquiry, joy).
2. **Fostering Critical AI Literacy as Collective World-Building: Curricular Models for Teaching With/About Generative AI** (Laurie Hurson) - The faculty development program and summer institute (teaching with, about, and against AI through disciplinary working groups, affinity discussions, tinkering, and mapping); four faculty interventions across City College, Hostos, and Brooklyn College; interventions for teaching critical AI literacy; student agency; and critical AI literacy as collective world-building (hooks, Barad, Haraway).
3. **Tinkering as Critical AI Literacy: Teaching AI Infrastructure through Breakdown and Reconfiguration** (Zach Muhlbauer) - Tinkering as recombination; eight tinkering moves in pedagogy; infrastructure and instructional design; teaching infrastructure through breakdown (Star); hyperparameters as instructional controls; AmigAI as breakdown and remediation; tinkering with system prompts across campuses; the CUNY AI Lab Sandbox.
4. **Beyond the Black Box: Resisting AI Inevitability Rhetoric and Implications for Science Education** (Şule Aksoy) - AI inevitability rhetoric as a knowledge claim deserving scientific scrutiny; a conceptual background in critical, transdisciplinary, and ecological approaches to science education; CALI as evidence through a qualitative case study of faculty reflections; three themes (critique of techno-determinism, threat to agency, material implications); implications for science education; conclusions framed through NASEM equity frames.
