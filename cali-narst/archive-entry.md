## Description

A related paper set bringing CALI's practices of community, transparency, and tinkering into the conference strand on technology in teaching. Luke Waltzer's paper situates the Institute in the Teaching and Learning Center, CUNY, its grounding scholarship, funding, goals, and strategy of asserting and preserving scholarly agency. Laurie Hurson's paper presents the curriculum, summer institute, and four faculty interventions as models of critical AI literacy as collective world-building. Zach Muhlbauer's paper argues for tinkering as critical AI literacy, teaching infrastructure through breakdown and reconfiguration (hyperparameters, AmigAI, system-prompt revisions across campuses, the CUNY AI Lab Sandbox). Şule Aksoy's paper treats AI inevitability rhetoric as a knowledge claim deserving scientific scrutiny, draws three themes from CALI faculty reflections, and sets out implications for science education.
