## 1. Title

Community, Transparency & Tinkering for Just Futures: Lessons Learned from the Critical AI Literacy Institute (CALI). CUNY Graduate Center, New York City. Domain-warp canvas.

## Luke Waltzer: The Critical AI Literacy Institute: Asserting and Preserving Scholarly Agency in the Age of AI (2–10)

- **About the Teaching and Learning Center:** educational development for graduate students; research and advocacy; open education and educational technology.
- **About CALI:** origins; elements (faculty development, research, research and development, advocacy).
- **Grounding Scholarship:** critical university studies; critical ed tech and infrastructure studies; DH, science education, educational development.
- **Defining Critical AI Literacy:** critical AI literacies (Guest, Suarez, and van Rooij, "Towards Critical Artificial Intelligence Literacies") and comprehensive AI literacies centered on agency.
- **The CUNY Context:** a large, federated system; heavy turnover at the central office; emphasis on career-connected learning; a minoritized population.
- **Our Goals:** reasoned adoption and informed refusal; communities of practice; research, tinker, advocate.

- **CALI's Strategy:** emphasize agency; identify small teaching moments; ground interventions in critical inquiry; joy.

## Laurie Hurson: Fostering Critical AI Literacy as Collective World-Building (11–21)

- **Overview:** CALI curriculum; faculty interventions; critical AI literacy as collective world-building.
- **The Critical AI Literacy Institute:** a faculty development program organized around disciplinary questions; developing a critical stance (Sano-Franchini, 2026); reflective, collaborative, interdisciplinary.
- **Summer Institute:** teaching with, about, and against AI; disciplinary working groups, affinity discussions, tinkering, mapping (Futch & Fine, 2012); goals of developing interventions, fostering a community of practice, and modeling critical inquiry (Lave & Wenger; Haraway; Harding).
- **Faculty Interventions:** Research and Information in the Digital Age (City College); Integrated Reading and Composition (Hostos Community College); Introduction to History and Literature (Brooklyn College); Statistical Methods in Earth and Atmospheric Sciences (City College).
- **Research and Information in the Digital Age:** an eight-class module on generative AI output assessment and model comparison, with surveys, discussion, group work, and reflective writing; topics from an introduction to LLMs through labor, environment, ethics, integrity, cognition, creativity, alternative models and resistance, and futures.
- **Integrated Reading and Composition:** creativity and labor; students' lived experiences and expertise; reflection on the value of writing.
- **Introduction to History and Literature:** readings on generative AI; a collaborative AI class policy; custom models for brainstorming and genre writing.
- **Statistical Methods in Earth and Atmospheric Sciences:** "What are all the ways that genAI influences my learning?"; an A/B test with a coding agent; a faculty reflection on moving from AI booster to a more nuanced stance.
- **Interventions for Teaching Critical AI Literacy:** frame AI as a sociotechnical system; consider values, power, and futures alongside skills; allow refusal and adoption; leverage community; explore knowledge production.
- **Critical AI Literacy & Student Agency:** the value of labor and friction; the future is not determined (Cottom, 2026); students can refuse, ask questions, and decide; give students control over their tools.
- **Critical AI Literacy, Agency, & Collective World-building:** hooks (the classroom as the most radical space of possibility), Barad (knowing as material practice), Haraway (vision from below); resist inevitability narratives; examine across scale; interrogate systems of power; build solidarity and resistance.

## Zach Muhlbauer: Tinkering as Critical AI Literacy (22–31)

- **Tinkering as Critical AI Literacy:** design proceeds through recombination, not invention from scratch; ends shift as materials reveal constraints; faculty work this way with models, prompts, datasets, and course materials; tinkering turns assembled parts into local, course-specific tools. (Langton's Ant canvas.)
- **Tinkering in Pedagogy:** raising questions; reflection-in-action (Schön); hands-on knowing; undirected exploration converging into focus (Resnick); problem framing; a personal toolbox; new applications; small design decisions that train judgment under incomplete knowledge.
- **Infrastructure and Instructional Design:** identifying pedagogical challenges; translating disciplinary methods into technical control over AI tools; prototyping domain-specific tools; pilots in Spanish (Hunter, Baruch) and English, History, and First-Year Writing (CCNY).
- **Teaching Infrastructure through Breakdown:** where does the model end and the interface begin? Consumer chat platforms obfuscate the difference; infrastructure becomes visible on breakdown (Star).
- **Hyperparameters as Instructional Controls:** temperature shapes the probability distribution; low is steady, high is loose; at 2.0 coherence breaks.
- **AmigAI: Toward Breakdown and Remediation:** a conversational language-learning tool on Gemma 3 27B piloted in heritage and non-native Spanish classes at Hunter and Baruch; flattens regional dialects into a dominant register; reveals the absence of low-resource languages in pretraining; underscores the value of fine-tuning.
- **Tinkering with System Prompts:** revisions tracked across campuses; settings shift with pedagogical goals; configuration as instructional design (Baruch: prompt rewrites as lesson plans; Hunter: prompt and parameters tuned together; Brooklyn: six Spaces in parallel, five models tested).
- **The CUNY AI Lab Sandbox:** a self-hosted Open WebUI instance; open-weight models small to large; only zero-retention providers with training off; side-by-side comparison, system prompts, parameters, transcript export; model cards tied to knowledge bases, skills, and tool calling; an AmigAI template compared with Gemma 3 27B.

## Şule Aksoy: Beyond the Black Box (32–42)

- **AI inevitability rhetoric as a knowledge claim deserving scientific scrutiny:** AI as a colonial project (Hao, 2025); as embodied and material technology (Crawford, 2021); as automated violence (Ricaurte, 2019).
- **Conceptual Background:** limited critique in science education; calls for critical transdisciplinary, transnational, and ecological approaches; critical consciousness and social justice in science education.
- **CALI as Evidence:** faculty reflections on inevitability; written reflections from online meetings on knowledge production, agency, and pedagogy; a qualitative case study.
- **Data Analysis:** initial coding, comparing codes, categorizing themes, memo writing, sharing the synthesis with faculty.
- **Theme 1, Critique of Techno-determinism:** faculty reflections on the pressure that inevitability creates and on the difficulty of responsible incorporation.
- **Theme 2, Threat to Agency:** reflections on "get on board or get left behind" and on pushing back against a single outcome for the technology.
- **Theme 3, Material Implications:** reflections on human and planetary damage and on the black-box problem of training data and bias.
- **Implications for Science Education:** epistemic agency; sensemaking; interdisciplinary thinking; socioscientific-issues-based instruction.
- **Conclusion:** reflections on the abstraction of planetary computation and on a preservice teacher's resolve to question frivolous use, framed through NASEM equity frames 4 and 5 (envisioning sustainable futures through STEM).
