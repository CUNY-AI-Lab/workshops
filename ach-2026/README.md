# Building Community-Oriented Infrastructures for AI Experimentation (ACH 2026)

_The CUNY AI Lab's panel at the Association for Computers and the Humanities 2026 conference: five presenters on CUNY's digital humanities context, the Critical AI Literacy Institute, the Sandbox, a classroom case study, and agentic tools for open university infrastructure._

**Event:** Association for Computers and the Humanities (ACH) 2026
**Date:** Friday, June 26, 2026, 12:00–1:15 p.m. CDT
**Format:** Conference panel (48 slides, five presenters, Q&A)
**Presenters:** Matthew K. Gold, Luke Waltzer, Zach Muhlbauer, Azucena García Gutiérrez, and Stephen Zweibel (CUNY Graduate Center)

_The deck was built on the CALI NARST theme and later remixed for the Lab's [October 14, 2026 session](../cail-oct-14/) and the [GCDI Orientation](../gcdi-orientation/)._

## About the CUNY AI Lab

The **CUNY AI Lab** is a faculty and staff-led incubator for experimentation with AI by CUNY researchers across disciplines, strengthening CUNY's position at the leading edge of technological innovation. Located at the CUNY Graduate Center and led by experienced scholars and technologists who have built widely used open publishing platforms, the Lab fosters momentum for AI experimentation while foregrounding ethical practices and values that reinforce CUNY's mission of accessible, equitable, and transformative education.

## Overview

The panel presents open classroom infrastructure that retains no user data and pairs the demonstrations with a critical discussion of corporate AI, misleading claims of openness, environmental costs, and the responsibilities of practical work in digital humanities. **Matthew K. Gold** sets the CUNY context (the largest urban public university, an engine of social mobility, a history of building open knowledge infrastructures such as the Academic Commons, Manifold, Debates in the Digital Humanities, and Commons In A Box) and the Graduate Center's draft AI Guidelines. **Luke Waltzer** tells the origin story of the Critical AI Literacy Institute: its grounding scholarship, four areas of intervention, goals (reasoned adoption and informed refusal, communities of practice, research, tinker, advocate), and two cohorts. **Zach Muhlbauer** presents the CUNY AI Lab Sandbox as an experimental learning infrastructure: precursors, zero-retention routing, side-by-side model comparison, purpose-built custom models, communities of practice, T(h)inkering with faculty fellows, and AmigAI. **Azucena García Gutiérrez** reports a classroom study of AmigAI in an intermediate Spanish course at Queens College, with student impressions and conclusions. **Stephen Zweibel** covers the Tools Portal, Agent Studio and Site Studio, a sixteen-week program teaching CUNY librarians to build with AI, the tools they built, PDF accessibility, Kale Workbench and Kale Deploy, and the path from an open model to a live app on CUNY-run infrastructure.
