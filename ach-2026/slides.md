## Matthew K. Gold: Approaching AI from a CUNY DH Perspective (2–8)

- **The CUNY Context:** the largest urban public university; the mission to educate "the children of the whole people" of New York City; the Graduate Center as a central node.
- **CUNY as an Engine of Social Mobility:** David Leonhardt's 2017 New York Times finding that CUNY propelled almost six times as many low-income students into the middle class and beyond as the eight Ivy League campuses plus Duke, MIT, Stanford, and Chicago combined.
- **DH at CUNY: Building Open Knowledge Infrastructures:** open-source platforms built in response to community needs, prioritizing open source and open access, alternatives to enterprise infrastructure (Academic Commons, Manifold, Debates in the Digital Humanities, Commons In A Box).
- **The Challenge:** "The Scandal of Digital Humanities" (Greenspan, Debates in the DH 2019).
- **AI at CUNY:** embed reflexive critique and a critical approach to technology; focus on classroom use; build alternatives to enterprise-level initiatives; preserve CUNY values (Critical AI Studies, DHUM 78000 / ENGL 89600, Fall 2025).
- **CUNY Graduate Center AI Guidelines:** expectations around accountability, collective responsibility, data privacy, right of refusal, and transparency; recommendations foregrounding a critical approach; ethical issues around commercial models; disclosure tools building on the Generative AI Delegation Taxonomy (GAIDeT). Draft last revised June 5, 2026.

## Azucena García Gutiérrez: AmigAI · Spanish 204 (26–35)

- **Classroom Context:** Intermediate Spanish, SPAN 204, Queens College; a mixed classroom of heritage and second-language learners; textbooks that lean on technologies ignoring diverse Latinx and Afrolatinx realities (Padilla & Vana, 2022; Vana & Padilla, 2024); building Critical Language Awareness (Leeman, 2018); using AmigAI to help students challenge texts from their own textbook.
- **The Configuration's Prompt:** a guide for discourse analysis of educational texts in Spanish at B2 level, for L2 and heritage learners, that never uses praise or validation and guides only through questions.
- **Interactions:** three textbook texts (immigration, El Caribe, Guatemalan Indigenous populations); AmigAI surfaces biases, stereotypes, omissions, and different realities through guided questions.
- **Classroom Application:** reading comprehension; task explanation and onboarding; individual interaction; debriefing discussion as a class.
- **First Impressions:** positive components (more intentional questioning, a before and an after the readings, AI critical awareness) and negative components (awareness of the learning process through AI, loss of a human presence, a monolingual approach that would not translate).
- **Conclusions:** a pedagogical tool for AI critical-literacy discussions; challenging under translingual approaches and at lower levels; conversations that develop critical awareness; technical issues and invisible labor remain real costs. The actual configuration (base model, system prompt, max tokens, top-k, top-p, temperature) shown as a JSON card.
- **Examples of Interactions:** a transcript analyzing "Corriente latina" with the student's written response.

## Stephen Zweibel: Harnessing Agentic AI: Tools for Open University Infrastructure (36–46)

- **What the Lab Builds and Runs:** how faculty and students build and run their own AI tools; the impact of agentic AI on research and libraries; from building tools to publishing them live.
- **Tools Portal:** standalone applications at tools.ailab.gc.cuny.edu; media tools (transcription, PDF accessibility, image description), a disclosure tool, and agentic studios; zero retention.
- **Working with Agentic AI:** Agent Studio and Site Studio; a shift from conversation toward task delegation; faculty and students build tools with minimal coding.
- **Teaching Librarians to Build with AI:** Agentic AI for Library Practice, a 16-week program for CUNY librarians with no programming background; build working tools with Claude Code, then investigate what it built; spot hallucinations and verify against the docs.
- **Tools CUNY Librarians Built:** harvested 133,000+ records from CUNY Academic Works; a daily-rebuilt RSS feed for a library journal; an OER spreadsheet turned into a searchable guide; flagged at-risk database access after one campus lost HeinOnline.
- **An Accessible Scholarly Record:** students run an accessibility check before depositing a dissertation; staff remediate library PDFs at scale with AI doing the first pass; built and run by the Lab for the Mina Rees Library.
- **Kale Workbench:** a coding agent in the browser on the Lab's Cloudflare infrastructure with open models; the Lab sets the model and the limits; nothing is pushed or published without approval.
- **Kale Deploy:** agents deploy through an MCP server; live at `<project>.cuny.qzz.io`, the first CUNY home for live web projects.
- **From Sandbox to a Live Web App:** Sandbox → Workbench → Kale Deploy, from an open model to a live app, hosted by the Lab for everyone at CUNY.
- **CUNY-Run AI Infrastructure:** together they let CUNY run its own AI infrastructure, built so the people who use it can inspect and operate it.
