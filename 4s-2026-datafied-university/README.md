## About the CUNY AI Lab

The **CUNY AI Lab** is a faculty and staff-led incubator for experimentation with AI by CUNY researchers across disciplines, strengthening CUNY's position at the leading edge of technological innovation. Located at the CUNY Graduate Center and led by experienced scholars and technologists who have built widely used open publishing platforms, the Lab fosters momentum for AI experimentation while foregrounding ethical practices and values that reinforce CUNY's mission of accessible, equitable, and transformative education.

This presentation brings the Lab's practice to a science and technology studies audience: how a public university's community-built AI infrastructure holds together commitment and doubt rather than resolving them.

## Overview

The paper takes techno-ambivalence, holding positive and negative evaluations of the same technology at once, as a condition to work with rather than a problem to resolve. It proposes a **ladder of ambivalence** that climbs from community to infrastructure: the same mixed stance appears among individuals deciding whether to use AI at all, in the communities of practice (the Critical AI Literacy Institute's cohorts, the Lab's fellows and workshop participants) that frame reasoned adoption and informed refusal as equally legitimate, and finally in the infrastructure itself, where the CUNY AI Lab builds and runs AI tools under constraints (zero data retention, open-weight models, a visible carbon estimate, criticality) that encode ambivalence into the system rather than leaving it to each user. Read through the panel's theme of the datafied university, the ladder is an argument about where ambivalence can be made durable and collective instead of private and exhausting.
