# Critical AI Literacy Institute at Brooklyn College

_A Teaching and Learning Center presentation on the Critical AI Literacy Institute (CALI): its origins, two faculty cohorts, curriculum and research, and the technical work that led to the CUNY AI Lab Sandbox._

This 27-slide presentation was delivered during a CALI faculty visit to Brooklyn College. It introduces CALI as a four-part intervention (faculty development, research, research and development, advocacy), profiles Cohort 1 (2025) and Cohort 2 (2026), walks through the three-phase curriculum and mixed-methods research program, and traces the "T(h)inkering" strand from Hugging Face pilots to the self-hosted CUNY AI Lab Sandbox. It closes with four domains for policy and strategy and a discussion of how CALI's frameworks, tools, and methods might translate to another campus.

**Date:** Wednesday, March 25, 2026
**Location:** Brooklyn College
**Format:** Presentation with discussion
**Presenters:** Luke Waltzer, Laurie Hurson, and Zach Muhlbauer (Teaching and Learning Center, CUNY Graduate Center)

_This presentation was delivered as part of CALI's faculty outreach; the Critical AI Literacy Institute is a Teaching and Learning Center program supported by a Google.org grant, and its technical strand is carried out with the CUNY AI Lab._

## About the CUNY AI Lab

The **CUNY AI Lab** is a faculty and staff-led incubator for experimentation with AI by CUNY researchers across disciplines, strengthening CUNY's position at the leading edge of technological innovation. Located at the CUNY Graduate Center and led by experienced scholars and technologists who have built widely used open publishing platforms, the Lab fosters momentum for AI experimentation while foregrounding ethical practices and values that reinforce CUNY's mission of accessible, equitable, and transformative education.

This presentation documents the Critical AI Literacy Institute, whose work brought the Graduate Center's AI efforts under one banner before the CUNY AI Lab launched in Fall 2025.

## Overview

The deck has four parts. **About CALI** names the conditions that prompted the Institute (inevitability discourse, CUNY's history of openness and innovation, the necessity of criticality, the need to slow things down) and summarizes two cohorts of 22 faculty across 12 CUNY campuses each. **Curriculum and Research** (Laurie Hurson) describes the spring-meetings, summer-institute, fall-implementation arc; the community of practice; and a mixed-methods study combining program evaluation, module documentation, and undergraduate surveys and interviews (over 400 responses). **T(h)inkering** (Zach Muhlbauer) covers the technical lead's role in translating disciplinary methods into control over AI tools, the Year 1 infrastructure on Hugging Face (Chat, Spaces, ChatUI-Helper, the AmigAI Spanish pilot at Hunter College), the move to a dedicated Open WebUI Sandbox for Cohort 2, five Cohort 2 projects, and lessons learned. **Looking Ahead** frames policy advocacy, instructional, labor, and institutional-strategy questions.

## Key Points

- **CALI as an intervention:** four interconnected areas of faculty development, research, research and development, and advocacy
- **Cohorts:** Cohort 1 (2025) and Cohort 2 (launching March 27, 2026), each 22 faculty across 12 campuses, spanning humanities, social science, and STEM
- **Curriculum:** reading, writing, and framing approaches in spring; an intensive summer institute with module development; teaching, research, and documentation in fall
- **Teaching critical AI literacy:** AI as a sociotechnical system; room for refusal and for adoption; community and collaboration as the vehicle for critical inquiry; the classroom as a space of possibility for agency and collective action
- **Then and now:** standalone Hugging Face Spaces for Cohort 1 pilots, hard to track or manage at scale; a self-hosted Sandbox with customizable model cards and multi-model comparison for Cohort 2
- **Lessons learned by doing:** tinkering as critical and methodological knowing; start small and A/B test; process as scaffolded progression and quality assurance; the accrued gains of open practices and community infrastructure
