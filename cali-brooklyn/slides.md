## Part 2: Curriculum and Research (Laurie Hurson)

### 11. Section break

### 12. Faculty Development: Three-Phase Arc

- **Spring meetings:** reading, writing, and framing approaches; establishing a community of practice; [critical pedagogical foundations](https://criticalai.commons.gc.cuny.edu/2025/08/23/critical-pedagogical-foundations/) and a technical interlude
- **Summer institute:** intensive exploration and discussion; module development and collaboration
- **Fall implementation:** teaching courses and modules; research and documentation

### 13. Community of Practice

Teaching with, about, and against AI. Mapping, disciplinary working groups, affinity discussions. Exploration and tinkering as shared methodology.

### 14. Mixed Methods

- **Faculty development data and program evaluation:** engagement, module development, and outcomes across the cohort
- **Critical AI literacy modules and teaching implementation:** how faculty translate CALI frameworks into course practice
- **Undergraduate student surveys and interviews:** "Describe a recent situation where you had to decide whether or not to use AI." "How do AI tools affect your learning process when you use them for school?"

### 15. Student Surveys

Over 400 responses, 2 interviews. (Stage: two survey charts.)

### 16. Faculty Interventions

Three early Cohort 1 pilots:

- **LIB 10000:** readings across scale, examining outputs, models
- **ENG 100:** reflective writing on creative labor and AI use
- **SOC 101:** applying core sociological concepts to AI

### 17. Teaching Critical AI Literacy...

- Frame AI as a sociotechnical system: cultural, labor-driven, non-neutral technology
- Allow for refusal and/or adoption of generative AI tools
- Pedagogy leverages community and collaboration to engage in critical inquiry
- Explore how knowledge is produced, for whom, and with what consequences

...as collective world-building: resist inevitability narratives; think across scale and contexts; interrogate systems of power and knowledge; center social, environmental, and epistemic justice; the classroom as a "radical space of possibility" for agency and collective action.

## Part 3: T(h)inkering (Zach Muhlbauer)

### 18. Section break

### 19. Technical Lead: Infrastructure and Instructional Design

1. Identifying **pedagogical challenges** faculty face in their classes
2. Translating disciplinary methods into **technical control over AI tools**
3. Prototyping **disciplinary AI tools** for classroom application
4. Pilots: **Spanish** (Hunter, Baruch); **English, History, First-Year Writing** (CCNY)

### 20. Year 1 Infrastructure: In Search of Shared Access

- **June 2025:** Hugging Face Chat (since sunsetted)
- **Summer 2025:** Hugging Face Spaces for hosting AI demos
- **ChatUI-Helper:** turning faculty configs into [web-hosted applications](https://huggingface.co/spaces/milwright/chatui-helper) (templates, identity, system config; example prompts, URL grounding, deployment)

### 21. Piloting on Hugging Face: Spanish at Hunter College, Fall 2025

- **AmigAI:** conversational Spanish practice
- Non-native and heritage-speaking Spanish classes at Hunter College
- **Built-in changelog** for retraceable system prompt edits

### 22. Then and Now: CALI Cohort 1, 2025

Faculty pilots as standalone Hugging Face Spaces; each demo configured and deployed individually with direct support; hard to track, evaluate, or manage at scale. (Stage: CALI tools collection on Hugging Face.)

### 23. Then and Now: CALI Cohort 2, 2026

- **Open WebUI:** open-source chat platform, self-hosted by the CUNY AI Lab
- **Dedicated sandbox** environment for teaching and learning
- Supports customization of **model cards** and comparison of **multiple models** at once

(Stage: the CUNY AI Lab Sandbox model selector; AmigAI conversational practice in Open WebUI; an AmigAI-Template versus Google Gemma comparison.)

### 25. Lessons Learned by Doing

1. **Tinkering** as critical and methodological knowing
2. Start small; leverage **A/B testing** to name what matters
3. Process as **scaffolded progression** and **quality assurance**
4. Accrued gains of **open practices** and **community infrastructure**

## Part 4: Looking Ahead and Discussion

### 26. Policy and Strategy: Four Domains

1. **Policy advocacy:** how do we preserve and protect the agency of teachers and students?
2. **Instructional:** how do we help faculty attend to the impacts of generative AI on student capacity to grow in their disciplines?
3. **Labor:** how do we attend to the labor impacts of generative AI for teachers, students, and staff?
4. **Institutional strategy:** how do we help constituencies develop coherent, values-driven frameworks to reckon with generative AI?
